Revision report

“Extremality shift of rotating black holes at finite Gauss–Bonnet coupling” — changes made on 28 September 2026 in response to prompts.txt and codex-review/.

The short version. The draft’s physics survived a severe independent audit: every printed identity I could check symbolically is correct, and the 13 couplings really are 13 independent nonlinear boundary-value solves, not the static potential replotted. Two defects were real. The source did not compile — $\alpha=\Nmaplist$ nests $…$ inside $…$ — and one number was labelled as a quantity it is not. Both are fixed.

The substantive work was to stop the paper over-claiming and to make its weakest step visible: the zero-temperature extrapolation now has its own figure, its own residual and drop-one diagnostics, and a full uncertainty budget; and Ψext now has a second, response-free determination from the Smarr relation. The headline is narrowed from a global minimum mass to the branch actually computed.

The resolution study is now done. The complete cold sequence and the complete T = 0 extrapolation were re-solved at N = 48 and N = 56 at every one of the 13 couplings — 26 sequences, plus 3 control walks at the production N = 64. The extremal intercept moves by at most 6.89×10−5, which is below the fit sensitivity at the same coupling in all 26 sequences and four orders of magnitude below the fall from π. Radial resolution is no longer an open gap in the paper.

1. What I verified before changing anything

The instruction was to be severe, and the specific doubt was whether figure 1 and the 13-coupling claim describe rotating numerical solutions or the static potential. They describe rotating solutions. work/manuscript_audit.py re-derives the paper’s algebra with sympy and asserts each difference vanishes, then reads the saved dataset back and asserts it against what the paper prints. It does not re-solve the boundary-value problem — the resolution study in §3 does that, the audit does not — so here the solver outputs are taken as given and only the claims about them are tested.

On the specific doubt: the dataset holds 356 accepted states across 13 couplings from α = 0 to 1/2, each carrying its own collocation resolution (64 or 96) and its own independently evaluated tensor residual (worst 9.76×10−9 relative). Every coupling reaches q > 0.5, so none of them is a static member. The six curves of the potential figure each carry 40–45 distinct spins. Nothing there is a rescaled static solution or a perturbation about α = 0.

2. The two defects

The manuscript source did not compilefixed

Section 4 contained namely $\alpha=\Nmaplist$, and the generated macro \Nmaplist already expands to $0$, $0.02$, …. The result is $…$0$…: TeX aborts with Display math should end with $$. This is why the committed main.pdf was a stale 15-page build that did not match its own source, which is the referee’s first and most serious complaint. The macro now carries the symbol in its first entry and the prose prints it as text.

manuscript/make_figures.py, manuscript/main.tex

A mislabelled integral discrepancyfixed

The paper said the discrepancy in the integral identity ∫Ψextdy = Δμ “across the full range” accumulates to 4.38×10−4. That is the largest running value, reached before the last interval and then partly cancelled. The value across the full range is the one at the last interval, 3.81×10−4. Both are now generated as separate macros and named for what they are.

Across the full range it accumulates to 4.38×10−4…
The discrepancy accumulated across the whole range, that is its value at the last interval y = 0.5112, is 3.81×10−4…; the largest running value, reached before the last interval and then partly cancelled, is 4.38×10−4.

3. The referee’s six main revisions

1. Freeze one authoritative versionpartly

The PDF is rebuilt from the current source and installed, so source, figures, tables and PDF now agree; the compile is clean, with no undefined references and no overfull boxes. The build is reproducible on Linux with Tectonic, recorded in manuscript/README.md; \pdfoutput=1 is now guarded so it no longer forces a pdfTeX driver a XeTeX engine cannot provide. The appendix states plainly that no number is typed into the source, so a rebuild reproduces the paper or fails.

Not done: no commit or archival identifier was created — the working agreement forbids committing unasked — and I did not adjudicate the KNOWN_EXCEPTIONS whitelist in checks/verify_recorded_digests.py, which needs the locked environment to re-record rather than a text edit.

2. Narrow the headline from a global minimum to the branch computeddone

We study how the minimum mass of a rotating black hole at fixed angular momentum changes… Thus the extremal mass increases monotonically while its slope does not.
We study how the extremal mass… on the equal-spin branch continuously connected to Myers–Perry… the central extrapolants of this slope stay positive… the extrapolated extremal mass correspondingly increases throughout while its slope does not.

The introduction now defines “the extremal branch” explicitly and disclaims uniqueness, stability and global minimality in the same paragraph. In the discussion the claim is restated at the precision the evidence supports, close to the referee’s own suggested wording. The overloaded word “spin” is separated into q, j, J and y by a new conventions table.

3. Resolution convergence at the important couplingsdone

The complete ascending walk and the complete T = 0 extrapolation were re-solved at N = 48 and N = 56 at every one of the 13 couplings: 26 independent sequences, each using the production start spin, the production acceptance gates and the production escalation rule, so that the base resolution is the only thing varied. The production side was not re-solved — it is the saved states re-extrapolated, which reproduces the shipped extremal table to 1.4×10−14 absolute, so only the coarse sides cost anything. experiments/egb_rotating_resolution_study.py, 29 walks, about 40 minutes.

At all 26 sequences the resolution difference is below the fit sensitivity already quoted for that coupling; the figure above draws the two together and the gap is two to five orders of magnitude. The valley at y = 0.1752, which the referee singled out because none of the three published comparisons was near it, moves by 7.7×10−7. Radial resolution is therefore not the leading term in the error budget; the extrapolation is. Going from N = 48 to N = 56 shrinks the difference at 9 of the 13 couplings.

Three caveats, all stated in the paper rather than smoothed over. The escalation rule lets the coldest states be accepted at N = 96 on both sides at 20 of the 26 sequences, where the comparison is weaker than the base resolutions suggest. A coarse walk need not reach as cold as the production one, so refitting both sides over a common temperature range isolates the resolution; that leaves 5.43×10−5. And the shipped measurement was produced under Python 3.11.9 / NumPy 2.4.6 / SciPy 1.17.1 while this study runs under 3.12.3 / 1.26.4 / 1.11.4, so each difference bounds the resolution change and the stack change together.

That last one is why I added an environment control: re-walking at the production N = 64 here, at α = 0.02, 0.15 and 0.5. It attributes up to 1.13×10−5 to the environment alone — not negligible, and at α = 1/2 larger than either resolution difference at the same coupling. The table entries are therefore upper bounds on the resolution effect, not estimates of it, and the true resolution sensitivity is smaller than the table says. The three comparisons recorded with the shipped measurement are free of this caveat, both sides having been computed in the same environment, and they are kept as table 9 for that reason.

Two incidental notes. The study script crashed twice at the JSON write, after all solves had finished — a nan from a relative difference taken against y = 0, then an inf from a trend ratio taken against an exactly vanishing difference, both rejected by allow_nan=False. Both now return None, and the disk memo meant neither crash cost a solve. And the condition number now states its norm (‖A‖2‖A−1‖2), its variables and that no scaling was applied, so it can no longer be read as comparable with another solver’s.

4. Treat the extremal extrapolation as the central modelling issuedone

A new figure 3 shows the step itself, which a table of intercepts hides: for each coupling, the six fitted states, the production cubic through them, the intercepts of all twenty competing fits drawn on the axis they land on, and the residuals in a second panel. The abscissa is the coordinate the fit actually uses, so thirteen windows spanning four decades of T can be compared. Two new diagnostics are generated and quoted: the production cubic describes its own points to 1.77×10−6 or better, and refitting with each of the six states dropped in turn moves the intercept by at most 1.09×10−5 — both far below every sensitivity in the extremal table, so the envelope reflects the length of the extrapolation and not a poor fit. Every extremal entry is now called a central extrapolant, in the abstract as well as the body.

Not done: the leading near-extremal powers of T are not derived. The paper now says so, in place of implying the cubic is a model.

5. An independent check of Ψdone

Read as an equation for Ψ rather than as a residual, the Smarr relation gives ΨSmarr = (2M − 3TS − 6ΩHJ)/2α, which uses only the charges of a single solution: no Jacobian, no linear solve, no parameter derivative, no second solution. It therefore tests the whole implicit-response machinery instead of repeating it. Over the 313 accepted states at α ≠ 0 it reproduces the response value to 1.45×10−6 relative; extrapolated to T = 0 by the same cubic, the two routes to Ψext agree to 1.59×10−6 at all 12 finite couplings (new table 5). The paper states what the two routes still share — the solutions and the asymptotic charge extraction — so the check is not oversold. The real finite-difference comparison now prints its locations, step sizes and the errors they leave (new table 2) instead of only the factor-of-four ratios.

6. A complete and readable uncertainty budgetdone

New table 7 gives the recorded spread of every extrapolated column, not just Ψext: δy, δμ, δσ from their own intercepts, and δj formed from each pair of extrapolated (M, J) intercepts so that their correlation is propagated rather than quadrature-summed, plus the two new fit diagnostics. The accompanying text names the effects the table does not separate — radial resolution, tail extraction, solver tolerance, the response solve, the quadrature — and says that the columns are not to be added. For the mass-curve check, propagating the recorded spreads of both routes puts the discrepancy inside the combined spread at all 12 intervals, the worst reaching 0.90 of it; the paper says in the same breath that the two routes share those spreads, so this is a consistency statement and not confirmation.

5. What I did not do

  • No independent solver. The resolution study varies the radial resolution inside the same code; it does not check that code against a different implementation.
  • No commit, no tag, no archival identifier. The working agreement says not to commit unasked.
  • The test suite was not run. pytest is not installed and the working agreement forbids installing into the base environment; the manuscript’s own checker (check_manuscript.py) and my audit both pass. tests/test_manuscript.py was updated to require the two new figures and the new data artifact.
  • Literature attributions were not re-verified. There is no papers/ directory in this tree, so the transcriptions from Wu–Lü, Ma–Li–Lü and Brihaye et al. rest on the manuscript’s own account. Their internal consistency was checked; their fidelity to the sources was not.
  • The near-extremal expansion was not derived, and the paper now says so instead of letting the cubic look like a model. It would do more for the quantitative claim than any further numerics, since it would replace the fitted cubic with a model.
  • No extremal bulk solution was constructed. Every extremal quantity remains a central extrapolant.
