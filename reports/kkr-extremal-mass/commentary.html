Is the extremal slope new?

On the manuscript's ∂Mext/∂α|J, compared with Kleihaus, Kunz & Radu, arXiv:2303.12471 (Ref. [2]).

Not ready to submit as it is.

Ref. [2] already contains Mext(α) and its slope, over about six times our coupling range. Our numbers agree with it. The paper needs reframing before it goes to a journal.

1. Mext is the observable; its slope adds little

Mext(J, α) is the physically relevant quantity. It is the extremality bound itself: it says which (M, J) a black hole can have at a given coupling. Its slope, compared with Mext, carries little new information:

  • The physics in the slope is its sign (the bound tightens) and its shape. Both are visible in Mext(α).
  • The slope is the natural object only at α → 0, where first-order perturbation theory gives π. At finite α, the comparison with perturbation theory is cleaner on Mext itself. At y = 0.51 the first-order formula predicts a shift 2.3 times the measured one, a mass 32% too high.
  • Its links to the entropy (Goon–Penco) and to Ψ in the extended first law are consistency identities. The manuscript itself declines to draw weak-gravity conclusions for uncharged, rotating holes. Ψ would be physical if α were set by a field such as a modulus, but the manuscript does not argue this.
  • Its practical value is technical: Ψ gives the slope pointwise, without differencing. That makes it a good tool and check for Mext, but not a result in itself.

2. Both can be read from Ref. [2]

How. Ref. [2] prints no Mext, but its Fig. 1 plots the extremal horizon area against α/M (main panel) and against J/M3/2 (inset). Matching the area between the two curves gives J/M3/2 as a function of α/M on the extremal branch, which is Mext(J, α). I took the curves from the PDF's vector data, and the entropy panels confirm the result.

Result. It agrees with ours to 2×10⁻³ in Mext and 0.015 in the slope, minimum included. Ref. [2] reaches α ≈ 2.6; we stop at 0.51.

3. Why Ref. [2] never stated it

This is my inference. Ref. [2] is a short survey of three families of solutions. Its question was whether the Einstein bound j ≤ 1 survives. That is weaker than knowing Mext(α): it only says the bound never relaxes. Like most higher-dimensional phase diagrams, its plots are normalised by the mass, so Mext(J) never appears as a plotted quantity. Ref. [2] does not cite the first-order result of Ma, Li and Lü, so it had no perturbative value to compare against. These authors also rarely give data tables.

4. Is it worth sending to a journal?

Not in its current form. Its headline (Mext grows, the slope dips and then rises) can be read from a figure in Ref. [2], and a referee who knows that paper would object. The abstract's claim that Ref. [2] gives "not Mext(J,α) or its slope" does not survive this comparison.

Yes, after two changes.

  • Reframe the paper as a precise, error-controlled confirmation of Ref. [2]. Lead with what Ref. [2] cannot give: Ψ on non-extremal solutions and its sign change, the implicit-response method, and the numerical check of Ma–Li–Lü. In that form it is a solid but modest paper for PRD or CQG.
  • For a stronger result, take the branch past x = 0.92 and test Ref. [2]'s conjecture that it ends at M = 3πα/4 with J → 0. That is the open question Ref. [2] leaves. Earlier work in this repo already reaches x ≈ 0.81.
